### Q1. What is a "harness" in the LLM-agent sense, and what is the earliest / most-cited definition of "harness engineering"?

**Takeaway**
The consensus formula is *agent = model + harness*: the harness is "everything in an AI agent except the model itself" — the loop, tools, system prompt/AGENTS.md, context management, permissions/sandbox, memory/state, and verification. The phrase "harness engineering" was coined (self-described) by Mitchell Hashimoto on 5 Feb 2026 and popularised six days later by OpenAI's 11 Feb 2026 post "Harness engineering: leveraging Codex in an agent-first world" (Ryan Lopopolo); the noun "harness" itself was already practitioner jargon in 2024–2025.

**Cited Findings — the term and its coinage**
- Hashimoto, "My AI Adoption Journey" (5 Feb 2026), section "Step 5: Engineer the Harness": he writes that there is no widely accepted industry name for the practice yet and that he has started calling it "harness engineering"; the idea is "anytime you find an agent makes a mistake" you "engineer a solution such that the agent never makes that mistake again." Fixes take two forms: "Better implicit prompting (AGENTS.md)" and "Actual, programmed tools" (scripts for screenshots, filtered tests, etc., usually with an AGENTS.md note so the agent knows they exist). Goal: "making an earnest effort for agents to be able to verify they're doing a Good Thing." — [mitchellh.com](https://mitchellh.com/writing/my-ai-adoption-journey)
- OpenAI, "Harness engineering: leveraging Codex in an agent-first world", by Ryan Lopopolo, 11 Feb 2026: a five-month internal experiment shipping an internal beta product with zero manually written lines of code — application logic, tests, CI config, docs, observability, internal tooling all written by Codex; roughly one million lines of code; estimated ~1/10 of the hand-written time. — [openai.com (search summary; page itself returns 403)](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/); corroborated by [InfoQ, 21 Feb 2026](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/02/openai-harness-engineering-codex)
- Quotes attributed to the OpenAI post by The Neuron: "From the agent's point of view, anything it can't access in-context while running effectively doesn't exist." and "Knowledge that lives in Google Docs, chat threads, or people's heads are not accessible to the system." — [The Neuron](https://www.theneuron.ai/explainer-articles/openais-harness-engineering-playbook-how-to-ship-1m-lines-of-code-without-writing-any/)
- InfoQ's summary of the OpenAI post: harness engineering moves engineers "away from implementing code and toward designing environments, specifying intent, and giving structured feedback"; techniques: a structured docs directory "containing maps, execution plans, and design specifications" as the agents' source of truth, cross-linked docs "mechanically enforced with linters and CI validation", a fixed dependency order "Types → Config → Repo → Service → Runtime → UI" checked by structural tests, agent-readable telemetry (logs/metrics/spans). Martin Fowler is quoted: "Harness includes context engineering, architectural constraints, and garbage collection." — [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/02/openai-harness-engineering-codex)
- Secondary stats on the OpenAI experiment: ~1,500 PRs over five months; Lopopolo on the OpenAI Build Hour said throughput reached "3-10 engineers' worth of throughput per person". — [The Neuron](https://www.theneuron.ai/explainer-articles/openais-harness-engineering-playbook-how-to-ship-1m-lines-of-code-without-writing-any/); Latent Space show notes say the repo "grew past a million lines across thousands of PRs" — [Latent Space, 7 Apr 2026](https://www.latent.space/p/harness-eng)
- Wikipedia "Agent harness": "The vocabulary of 'harness engineering' emerged in early 2026, and attribution of the specific phrase is contested." Several accounts credit Hashimoto's Feb 2026 post; others credit Vivek Trivedy of LangChain ("Anatomy of an Agent Harness"); "A widely cited OpenAI engineering report" plus Thoughtworks, LangChain and Anthropic follow-ups "helped spread the term." Also: "The nouns *agent harness* and *LLM harness* circulated among practitioners before the associated discipline was named." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_harness)
- Chinese secondary account (新浪科技转载 "AI信息Gap", 14 Apr 2026) gives the same chronology: Hashimoto's 5 Feb post, OpenAI's engineering blog "6 days later" using the title, then Anthropic and Martin Fowler following; it notes Hashimoto himself said he was unsure the name was widely adopted. — [finance.sina.cn](https://finance.sina.cn/2026-04-14/detail-inhumfsm9224998.d.html?vt=4)
- arXiv 2609.00006 (15 Jul 2026) states harness engineering "was named as a discipline in early 2026". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00006)

**Cited Findings — definitions of "harness" (quote bank, chronological)**
- Simon Willison, "The 'think' tool" (21 Mar 2025), pre-coinage usage: "an external harness goes away and executes the specified tool and feeds the result back into the conversation." — [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Mar/21/the-think-tool/)
- Anthropic, "Effective harnesses for long-running agents" (26 Nov 2025, Justin Young): no formal definition; describes the Claude Agent SDK as "a powerful, general-purpose agent harness adept at coding"; a footnote says the "overall agent harness was otherwise identical" between initializer and coding agents, only the prompt differed. — [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents)
- Gabriel Chua (OpenAI DevEx APAC), quoted by Simon Willison (22 Feb 2026): the harness is "the collection of instructions and tools"; "Codex models are trained in the presence of the harness. The harness, in turn, is shaped around how the model plans, invokes tools, and recovers from failure." Willison: "the first acknowledgment I've seen from an OpenAI insider that the Codex model family are directly trained for the Codex harness." — [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/22/how-i-think-about-codex/)
- Latent Space AINews "Is Harness Engineering real?" (5 Mar 2026): a harness is "the layer that connects, protects, and orchestrates components — without doing the work itself." — [latent.space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-is-harness-engineering-real)
- LangChain, Vivek Trivedy, "Anatomy of an Agent Harness" (11 Mar 2026 per secondary report): "Agent = Model + Harness"; harness = everything that isn't the model — system prompts, tool execution, orchestration logic, middleware hooks; filesystems and sandboxes as key primitives. — [blockchain.news summary](https://blockchain.news/news/langchain-agent-harness-architecture-framework) (primary LangChain URL could not be fetched; see Gaps). Trivedy's slogan per Addy Osmani: "Agent = Model + Harness. If you're not the model, you're the harness." — [addyosmani.com](https://addyosmani.com/blog/agent-harness-engineering/)
- Simon Willison, "How coding agents work" (Agentic Engineering Patterns, 16 Mar 2026): "A coding agent is a piece of software that acts as a harness for an LLM". — [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/guides/agentic-engineering-patterns/how-coding-agents-work/)
- Birgitta Böckeler (Thoughtworks), "Harness engineering for coding agent users", martinfowler.com (2 Apr 2026): "harness" has emerged as shorthand for "everything in an AI agent except the model itself"; a *user harness* = "guides and sensors" built for a specific coding-agent use case. — [martinfowler.com](https://martinfowler.com/articles/harness-engineering.html)
- Addy Osmani, "Agent Harness Engineering" (19 Apr 2026): harness = "every piece of code, configuration, and execution logic that isn't the model itself"; "coding agent = AI model(s) + harness"; harness engineering treats the harness "as a real artifact" and tightens it each time the agent fails. — [addyosmani.com](https://addyosmani.com/blog/agent-harness-engineering/)
- Hugging Face agent glossary (25 May 2026, Paniego & Gosthipaty): Harness = "The execution layer inside the agent: it calls the model, handles its tool calls, decides when to stop"; notes products often use "harness" for everything that isn't the model; "An agent is a model plus everything around it that lets it act, not just respond"; harness engineering = "the discipline of designing this layer well." — [huggingface.co](https://huggingface.co/blog/agent-glossary)
- arXiv 2605.23950 (7 May 2026): harness = "the software layer between the model and the task that constructs the context the model sees" / "the infrastructure layer that governs context construction, tool interaction, orchestration, and verification". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23950v1)
- Lilian Weng, "Harness Engineering for Self-Improvement" (4 Jul 2026): harness = "the system surrounding a base model that orchestrates execution" — deciding how the model thinks/plans, calls tools, manages context, stores artifacts, evaluates results; harness engineering "additionally include[s] workflow design (e.g. loop engineering), evaluation, permission controls, and persistent state management" and is closer to runtime/software-system design than to prompt templates. — [lilianweng.github.io](https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-07-04-harness/)
- arXiv 2609.00006 (15 Jul 2026, Barbaste et al., source-code study of 11 harnesses: Claude Code, Codex CLI, Gemini CLI, Mistral Vibe, OpenHands, Aider, Mini-SWE-Agent, Hermes, Pi, OpenCode, OpenClaw): agent = model + harness, harness = "the runtime that couples an LLM to the world" (loop, tools, context management, safety controls, orchestration, extension surfaces); harness engineering = "the design and evolution of that runtime"; seven canonical subsystems, 29 design patterns; no studied runtime imports a general-purpose agent framework, none uses vector-embedding code retrieval; Skills adopted in 9/11 vs MCP 8/11. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00006)
- Wikipedia component list: "tool dispatch, memory and state persistence, an isolated sandbox or workspace, context management," plus guardrails such as scoped permissions, approval tiers and monitoring. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_harness)
- OpenAI open-sourced "Harness" (the Codex execution engine) on 20 Aug 2026 under Apache-2.0: `codex exec` CLI, the Codex SDK, and `app-server`; described as "the core underlying engine and execution framework that powers its flagship coding agent, Codex", managing "the execution loop for AI agents, including task comprehension, long-conversation memory retention," event streaming, tool invocation, interruptibility, status sync and human-in-the-loop approvals. — [OpenSourceForU, 21 Aug 2026](https://www.opensourceforu.com/2026/08/openai-open-sources-codex-harness-framework) (single secondary source; not verified against OpenAI materials)

**Inferences**
- Two layers of the word coexist: (a) the narrow *runtime* sense (the loop that calls the model, dispatches tools, decides when to stop — HF glossary, arXiv 2609.00006) and (b) the broad *everything-but-the-model* sense (Böckeler, Osmani, Trivedy). OpenAI's post uses a third, *repo-level* sense: the harness is the codebase's own docs, linters, structural tests and telemetry that the agent lives inside. A tutorial should name all three explicitly.
- The "canonical" definition for citation purposes is Hashimoto's (date-first, self-declared coinage) for the *practice*, and Trivedy/Osmani's "Agent = Model + Harness" for the *noun*. OpenAI's post is the most-cited *popularising* document but, per the sources fetched, does not contain a one-sentence definition.

### Q2. How do Anthropic's posts define/characterise the harness (Dec 2024 → 2026)?

**Takeaway**
Anthropic never gives a one-line definition. The Dec 2024 and Sep 2025 posts do not use the word "harness" at all (they talk about "augmented LLM", "agent-computer interface" and "context engineering"); the Nov 2025 and Mar 2026 posts use "harness" as an unexplained noun for the surrounding system and contribute the strongest *design-philosophy* statements: every harness component "encodes an assumption about what the model can't do on its own," and the harness should be pruned when a new model arrives.

**Cited Findings**
- "Building effective agents" (19 Dec 2024, Erik Schluntz & Barry Zhang): "Workflows are systems where LLMs and tools are orchestrated through predefined code paths." / "Agents, on the other hand, are systems where LLMs dynamically direct their own processes and tool usage". Building block: "The basic building block of agentic systems is an LLM enhanced with augmentations such as retrieval, tools, and memory." Appendix 2 urges teams to "invest just as much effort in creating good agent-computer interfaces (ACI)" as in HCI; summary principle: "Carefully craft your agent-computer interface (ACI) through thorough tool documentation and testing." Neither "harness" nor "scaffold" appears. — [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)
- "Effective context engineering for AI agents" (29 Sep 2025): "Prompt engineering refers to methods for writing and organizing LLM instructions for optimal outcomes." / "Context engineering refers to the set of strategies for curating and maintaining the optimal set of tokens…" / "we view context engineering as the natural progression of prompt engineering." Neither "harness" nor "scaffolding" appears. — [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
- "Effective harnesses for long-running agents" (26 Nov 2025, Justin Young): "The core challenge of long-running agents is that they must work in discrete sessions." Compaction "isn't sufficient." Harness components: an *initializer agent* (writes `init.sh`, a `claude-progress.txt` progress log, initial git commit), a *coding agent* for each later session, a JSON *feature list* with all features initially failing (JSON chosen because models alter it less than Markdown), progress notes + git history for fresh-session orientation, browser automation (Puppeteer MCP) for end-to-end testing — "Providing Claude with these kinds of testing tools dramatically improved performance." Git lets the model "revert bad code changes and recover working states." Claude Agent SDK called "a powerful, general-purpose agent harness adept at coding." — [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents)
- "Harness design for long-running application development" (24 Mar 2026, Prithvi Rajasekaran, Anthropic Labs): "harness design has a substantial impact on the effectiveness of long running agentic coding"; "every component in a harness encodes an assumption about what the model can't do on its own"; "the space of interesting harness combinations doesn't shrink as models improve." Architecture: planner ("took a simple 1-4 sentence prompt and expanded it into a full product spec"), generator (feature-by-feature sprints; sprint construct later removed), evaluator ("used the Playwright MCP to click through the running application the way a user would"; GAN-inspired generator/discriminator split so the builder does not grade its own work). *Context reset* = "clearing the context window entirely and starting a fresh agent" with a structured hand-off, vs compaction which summarises in place — "A reset provides a clean slate." Resets were needed for Sonnet 4.5's "context anxiety"; with Opus 4.5 "I was able to drop context resets from this harness entirely." Guidance: when a new model arrives, strip away "pieces that are no longer load-bearing to performance"; "find the simplest solution possible, and only increase complexity when needed"; "the scaffold surrounding the model matters less over time" (in some cases). — [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps); date/author corroborated by [InfoQ, Apr 2026](https://infoq.com/news/2026/04/anthropic-three-agent-harness-ai/)
- Claude Code's own self-description sits on the *thin-harness* side: Boris Cherny — "this is the thinnest possible wrapper over the model"; Cat Wu — "It is very much the simplest thing I think by design." — [Latent Space AINews, 5 Mar 2026](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-is-harness-engineering-real)
- Simon Willison on Claude Code bugs (24 Apr 2026): "three separate issues in the Claude Code harness caused complex but material problems"; "the kinds of bugs that affect harnesses are deeply complicated". — [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Apr/24/recent-claude-code-quality-reports/)

**Inferences**
- Anthropic's trajectory is terminological: "augmented LLM"/ACI (2024) → "context engineering" (2025) → "harness" as a first-class noun (Nov 2025 onward). Their contribution to the *definition* is small; their contribution to the *philosophy* ("components encode model deficits; prune per model generation") is the most-quoted counterweight to "build a big harness".

**Gaps**
- No Anthropic post found that defines "harness engineering" as a phrase; searches for a 2026 Anthropic post with that exact title returned only third-party claims (e.g., "Anthropic published 3 papers on it") that could not be verified.

### Q3. What did SWE-agent (ACI) and Terminal-Bench / Harbor contribute?

**Takeaway**
SWE-agent (May 2024) supplied the intellectual precursor — the *agent-computer interface*, i.e. the idea that LM agents are end users who "would benefit from specially-built interfaces" — and the first widely reproduced evidence that interface design, not just the model, drives SWE-bench scores. Terminal-Bench 2.0 / Harbor (Jan 2026) operationalised the separation by making *agent* (e.g. Claude Code, Codex, Terminus) and *model* independent flags inside a shared evaluation harness, so leaderboards report the pair.

**Cited Findings**
- SWE-agent, arXiv 2405.15793 (v1 6 May 2024; v3 11 Nov 2024), John Yang, Carlos E. Jimenez, Alexander Wettig, Kilian Lieret, Shunyu Yao, Karthik Narasimhan, Ofir Press: LM agents as end users "would benefit from specially-built interfaces to the software they use"; "SWE-agent's custom agent-computer interface (ACI) significantly enhances an agent's ability to create and edit code files"; pass@1 of 12.5% on SWE-bench and 87.7% on HumanEvalFix, "far exceeding the previous state-of-the-art achieved with non-interactive LMs." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15793)
- Anthropic's Dec 2024 post adopted the ACI term as a design principle (see Q2). — [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)
- Terminal-Bench 2.0, arXiv 2601.11868 (17 Jan 2026), Mike A. Merrill, Alexander G. Shaw, Nicholas Carlini … Ludwig Schmidt (85 authors): "a carefully curated hard benchmark composed of 89 tasks" in terminal environments, each with its own environment, human-written solution and tests; frontier models and agents score below 65%; the dataset and *evaluation harness* are released. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11868)
- tbench.ai docs: "Terminal-Bench runs in Harbor"; a run selects agent and model independently (`-a claude-code`, `-m anthropic/claude-sonnet-5`), the task set (`-d terminal-bench/terminal-bench@4.0.0`) and the sandbox (`-e modal`). — [tbench.ai/docs](https://www.tbench.ai/docs). Harbor is described as "the official harness for Terminal-Bench 2.0" and as the rewrite of the earlier `harness/` package; the leaderboard records which agent harness is used. — [search summary of tbench.ai/Harbor README](https://www.tbench.ai/docs/run-terminal-bench-2-0)
- Terminal-Bench is the benchmark most used to *demonstrate* harness effects: LangChain's Terminal-Bench 2.0 rise from 52.8 to 66.5 with the model unchanged (environmental context, time awareness, loop detection, build-verify-fix cycles) — [ai.engineer speaker bio for Vivek Trivedy](https://www.ai.engineer/speakers/vivek-trivedy); AHE harness evolution moving GPT-5.4 (high) from 69.7% to 77.0% on Terminal-Bench 2 — [arXiv 2605.23950](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23950v1); the ICML 2026 "Scaffold Effect" study on Terminal-Bench Pro — [icml.cc](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/82739).

**Inferences**
- Terminology mapping: Terminal-Bench uses "harness" for the *evaluation* infrastructure (Harbor) and "agent" for what the rest of the discourse calls the harness (Claude Code, Codex CLI, Terminus). When reading leaderboards, "agent" ≈ "model-facing harness". A tutorial should warn readers about this double use.

**Gaps**
- SWE-agent's ablation table (e.g., effect of removing the file viewer window, search tools, or linting on resolve rate) was not retrievable from the abstract page; the fetch only returned headline numbers. The paper does contain such ablations, but no exact figures are cited here.
- No tbench.ai FAQ text explicitly explaining *why* agent and model are reported separately was found.

### Q4. How does "harness engineering" relate to prompt engineering, context engineering, agent scaffolding, ACI, and agentic engineering?

**Takeaway**
Most 2026 sources nest the terms: prompt engineering ⊂ context engineering ⊂ harness engineering (the harness is the *delivery mechanism* for context), with "scaffold/scaffolding" treated as a near-synonym of harness (or, in the Hugging Face glossary, the behaviour-defining sub-layer of it), ACI as the 2024 precursor focused on tool/interface design, and Karpathy's "agentic engineering" as the broader *human role* of orchestrating agents rather than a layer of the system. Böckeler is the notable dissent: she places harness engineering *inside* context engineering.

**Cited Findings**
- Anthropic (29 Sep 2025): context engineering is "the natural progression of prompt engineering" and covers "all the other information that may land there outside of the prompts." — [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
- Manus, "Context Engineering for AI Agents: Lessons from Building Manus" (Yichao "Peak" Ji, 18 Jul 2025): KV-cache hit rate is "the single most important metric for a production-stage AI agent"; Manus input:output token ratio ≈ 100:1; cached input $0.30/MTok vs $3/MTok uncached (Claude Sonnet); keep prompt prefix stable (no second-precision timestamps), append-only context, deterministic serialisation; mask tool logits rather than remove tools mid-task. — [manus.im](https://manus.im/blog/Context-Engineering-for-AI-Agents-Lessons-from-Building-Manus); date per [dev.to summary](https://dev.to/contextspace_/context-engineering-for-ai-agents-key-lessons-from-manus-1lgb)
- Latent Space episode framing (7 Apr 2026): "Move over, context engineering. Now it's time for Harness engineering." — [latent.space](https://www.latent.space/p/harness-eng)
- Addy Osmani (19 Apr 2026): harnesses are "largely delivery mechanisms for good context engineering"; scaffolding used as a near-synonym for harness. — [addyosmani.com](https://addyosmani.com/blog/agent-harness-engineering/)
- Böckeler / martinfowler.com (2 Apr 2026): "Engineering a user harness for a coding agent is a specific form of context engineering"; guides = feedforward controls that steer "before it acts", sensors = feedback controls that observe after; each can be computational (linters, tests) or inferential (AI review, LLM-as-judge). — [martinfowler.com](https://martinfowler.com/articles/harness-engineering.html)
- Hugging Face glossary (25 May 2026): Harness = execution layer ("calls the model, handles its tool calls, decides when to stop"); Scaffolding = "The behavior-defining layer around the model" (system prompt, tool descriptions, response parsing), with "scaffold" also used loosely for supporting infrastructure; Context engineering = "Designing what goes into the agent's context window: what the model sees at each step." — [huggingface.co](https://huggingface.co/blog/agent-glossary)
- Lilian Weng (4 Jul 2026): harness engineering goes beyond prompts to "workflow design (e.g. loop engineering), evaluation, permission controls, and persistent state management"; prompt engineering is the historical parallel — manual tricks faded but "the need to specify goals and context will not disappear." — [lilianweng.github.io](https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-07-04-harness/)
- Chinese explainer nesting (nxcode.io, 1 Mar 2026): prompt engineering operates on a single interaction; context engineering on what the model sees in its window; harness engineering on the whole agent system (environment, constraints, feedback, lifecycle) — it "包含" context engineering and "借鉴了" prompt engineering. — [nxcode.io](https://www.nxcode.io/zh/resources/news/harness-engineering-complete-guide-ai-agent-codex-2026)
- ACI: see Q3 (SWE-agent 2024; Anthropic Dec 2024 "Carefully craft your agent-computer interface (ACI)"). — [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)
- "Agentic engineering": Karpathy's February 2026 post (one-year anniversary of "vibe coding", which he coined Feb 2025) argued "agentic engineering" better captures the next stage of AI-assisted development; reported wording: the new default is that you are not writing the code directly 99% of the time, you orchestrate agents who do and act as oversight, and "engineering" signals there is an art and science to it. Some outlets place the fuller push at Sequoia AI Ascent in April 2026. — [AOL/Business Insider](https://www.aol.com/articles/man-coined-vibe-coding-says-203029543.html); [noqta.tn](https://noqta.tn/en/news/karpathy-hasnt-coded-since-december-ai-agents-psychosis-2026); [tianpan.co](https://tianpan.co/forum/t/karpathy-says-vibe-coding-is-passe-at-its-one-year-anniversary-agentic-engineering-is-the-new-paradigm/628)
- Simon Willison adopted Karpathy's term for his serialized guide "Agentic Engineering Patterns" (chapters from 25 Feb 2026 onward), whose "How coding agents work" chapter (16 Mar 2026) defines the agent *as* the harness: "A coding agent is a piece of software that acts as a harness for an LLM". — [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/guides/agentic-engineering-patterns/how-coding-agents-work/)
- Karpathy's January 2026 Claude Code thread described "a programmable layer of abstraction" of agents, subagents, prompts, contexts, memory, modes, permissions, tools, plugins, skills, hooks, MCP — harness vocabulary without the word. — [codewithandrea newsletter](https://codewithandrea.com/newsletter/january-2026/)
- Arize pushes back on calling frameworks like LangGraph "harnesses": they are frameworks for humans to build agents, not harnesses. — [arize.com](https://arize.com/blog/what-is-an-agent-harness/)
- HumanLayer / Dex Horthy, "12-Factor Agents" (talk Aug 2025; repo github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents): production agents are "mostly deterministic software with targeted LLM steps", own your control flow, contact humans via tool calls — a harness-design manifesto that does not use the word "harness". — [ai.engineer](https://ai.engineer/talks/12-factor-agents); [MLOps Community](https://home.mlops.community/public/videos/12-factor-agents-patterns-of-reliable-llm-applications-dexter-horthy-agents-in-production-2025-2025-08-06). Osmani cites HumanLayer's view that most agent failures are configuration problems rather than model-weight problems. — [addyosmani.com](https://addyosmani.com/blog/agent-harness-engineering/)

**Inferences**
- A clean one-slide taxonomy consistent with the majority of sources: *Prompt engineering* = the words; *Context engineering* = which tokens are in the window at each step; *Harness engineering* = the code that decides the loop, tools, permissions, state, verification (and therefore produces the context); *ACI* = the 2024 name for the tool/interface slice of the harness; *Scaffold* = older/loose synonym for harness; *Agentic engineering* = the human practice of working through agents (Karpathy/Willison), of which harness engineering is one skill.
- The Böckeler inversion (harness ⊂ context) is coherent if "context engineering" is read as "everything that determines what the model sees", but it is the minority usage.

**Gaps**
- No primary Karpathy post text retrieved (X not fetchable); wording is from press paraphrase. No Karpathy statement using the word "harness" was found.

### Q5. Evidence and arguments that the harness changes outcomes — and the counter-arguments

**Takeaway**
Evidence is strong that the same model can swing by 10–35 points across harnesses on SWE-bench-style and Terminal-Bench leaderboards, and that harness-induced variance can exceed model-induced variance ("Binding Constraint Thesis"); but controlled studies with *comparable, well-built* harnesses find pass-rate gaps of only 0–8 points (with up to 40× token-cost differences), and Scale/METR data suggest near-noise differences between Claude Code/Codex and a basic scaffold. The debate is framed as "Big Model vs Big Harness": Noam Brown / Claude Code's "thinnest wrapper" / bitter-lesson critics vs. "the harness is the product" / Lopopolo / Trivedy; the synthesis position (Anthropic Labs, Lilian Weng, Osmani) is that harnesses get *internalised* and re-shaped rather than eliminated.

**Cited Findings — same model, different harness**
- Position paper "Stop Comparing LLM Agents Without Disclosing the Harness" (arXiv 2605.23950, 7 May 2026; Zhang, Wang, Ge, Xu, Hamm, Reddy): the harness "is often a stronger determinant of agent performance than the model it wraps"; "harness-induced variance can substantially exceed model-induced variance, including cases of model ranking reversal"; "leaderboard comparisons for long-horizon agents should be treated as incomplete and potentially misleading." Numbers compiled in the paper: SWE-bench Pro, Claude Opus 4.5: SEAL scaffold 45.9% → Claude Code 55.4%; SWE-bench Verified Mini (HAL): Claude Sonnet 4.5 SWE-Agent 68% vs HAL Generalist 34%, GPT-5 Medium 46% vs 12%, o4-mini gap ≈ 48 pts; SWE-bench Verified: Grok 4 SWE-agent 58.6% vs xAI scaffold 72–75%; Epoch AI: scaffold-only variation up to 11 pts (GPT-5) and 15 pts (Kimi K2 Thinking); Terminal-Bench 2: GPT-5.4 (high) 69.7% → 77.0% via AHE harness evolution; a fixed model 52.8% → 66.5% via prompt+middleware+verification (Trivedy/Meta-Harness/AHE); Vercel: cutting a tool set from 15 to 2 tools raised success 80% → 100%. **Cited Findings — the pro-harness arguments ("the harness is the product")**
- "The Harness is the Product" (X post by Hxlfed14, cited Mar 2026): every production agent shares one core loop — call the model, execute tools, append results, repeat — so differentiation lives in the harness. — [latent.space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-is-harness-engineering-real)
- Jerry Liu: "the biggest barrier to getting value from AI is your own ability to context and workflow engineer the models." — [latent.space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-is-harness-engineering-real)
- Lopopolo (Latent Space, 7 Apr 2026): "The only fundamentally scarce thing is the synchronous human attention of my team"; bottleneck shifted from tokens to human attention; calls it borderline "negligent" not to spend >~1B tokens/day (~$2–3k/day); Symphony = Elixir orchestration layer for many Codex agents; "ghost libraries" = software distributed as a spec that agents reimplement. — [latent.space](https://www.latent.space/p/harness-eng)
- Codex co-training: "Codex models are trained in the presence of the harness" (Chua, via Willison, 22 Feb 2026) — the harness is part of what the model is optimised for, which is why swapping harnesses changes scores. — [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/22/how-i-think-about-codex/)
- Trivedy/LangChain: moved a coding agent from outside the top 30 to the top 5 on Terminal-Bench 2.0 "by changing only the harness" (company's own account). — [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/langchain-agent-harness-architecture-framework); [addyosmani.com](https://addyosmani.com/blog/agent-harness-engineering/)
- Tom Tunguz, "aftermarket harnesses": third-party harnesses can outperform the model vendor's own. — [tomtunguz.com](https://tomtunguz.com/aftermarket-harnesses/index.md)

**Cited Findings — the counter-arguments ("the model eats the harness" / bitter lesson)**
- Noam Brown (as quoted in Latent Space AINews): "those scaffolds will also just be replaced by the reasoning models"; a unified model should not need a router. 品玩 paraphrases him as holding that "Harness本质是拐杖" (a crutch) that models will outgrow. — [latent.space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-is-harness-engineering-real); [pingwest](https://www.pingwest.com/a/312628)
- Claude Code leads: "the thinnest possible wrapper over the model" (Boris Cherny); "the simplest thing … by design" (Cat Wu). — [latent.space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-is-harness-engineering-real)
- Anthropic Labs (24 Mar 2026): "every component in a harness encodes an assumption about what the model can't do on its own"; context resets dropped entirely with Opus 4.5; prune "pieces that are no longer load-bearing". — [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)
- Traversal, "The Bitter Lesson for Agent Harnesses": harness builders act as if the bitter lesson does not apply; the usual fix of splitting work into smaller pieces with an up-front controller "usually makes the agent worse". — [traversal.com](https://www.traversal.com/blog/ai-agent-architecture-mistakes)
- dyb, "The bitter lesson for harness builders: why I am destroying my beautiful machine": three years of structured harness called wrong; encoding prior knowledge is "a never-ending fool's errand"; favours meta-methods. — [dyb.mataroa.blog](https://dyb.mataroa.blog/blog/the-bitter-lesson-for-harness-builders-why-i-am-destroying-my-beautiful-machine/)
- Daniel Miessler, "Good and Bad Harness Engineering": bad = prescriptive step-by-step instructions that try to outsmart the model; good = environment and verification. — [danielmiessler.com](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/good-and-bad-harness-engineering)
- Latent Space AINews conclusion: both camps "have an incentive to sell their own layer"; harness engineering has real value "while still respecting the Bitter Lesson". — [latent.space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-is-harness-engineering-real)
- Synthesis positions: Osmani calls "better models make harnesses obsolete" the "naive story" — the design space shifts rather than shrinks, though some scaffolding becomes dead code (Opus 4.6 largely removed Sonnet 4.5's context anxiety); a model–harness training loop post-trains harness primitives into models, but co-training causes overfitting so swapping tools can regress. — [addyosmani.com](https://addyosmani.com/blog/agent-harness-engineering/). Harrison Chase: much 2023 scaffolding is gone "but this has been replaced by other types of scaffolding"; memory and tool use will not dissolve. — [neura.market summary](https://www.neura.market/news/agent-harness-absorbed-by-models-what-comes-next). Lilian Weng: "many harness improvements will be internalized into core model behavior" but "the interface with external context and tools should remain". — [lilianweng.github.io](https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-07-04-harness/). Chinese commentary (heyuan110, 8 May 2026) titles the same view "Window of Opportunity, Not a Forever Moat". — [heyuan110.com](https://www.heyuan110.com/posts/ai/2026-05-08-harness-engineering-window-of-opportunity/)
- Reading list for the debate (Hugo Bowne-Anderson, "The agent harness reading list"). — [notion.site](https://open-racer-a67.notion.site/The-agent-harness-reading-list-35e14bb7e4a2805d881ae261573ff76f)

**Inferences**
- The evidence splits by comparison type: *bad vs good* harness (HAL Generalist vs SWE-Agent; minimal vs full) shows 10–35-point swings; *good vs good* harness (Claude Code vs SWE-Agent on SWE-Atlas; Goose/OpenCode/OpenHands) shows ≤8 points on pass rate but order-of-magnitude differences in cost/latency. So "the harness matters" is best defended on (1) floor-raising, (2) cost/tokens, (3) long-horizon and real-repo tasks, rather than on headline SWE-bench Verified deltas between mature harnesses.
- The "model eats the harness" argument and the "harness is the product" argument are compatible if one accepts co-training: the harness is where today's model deficits are patched, and those patches migrate into the next model (Codex trained "in the presence of the harness"), so the *content* of a harness has a half-life while the *role* persists.

**Gaps**
- No METR primary publication with numbers was found; the METR claim is a tweet relayed by Latent Space.
- The much-repeated "46% vs 80% same model, different harness (Cursor)" figure could not be traced to a primary source; closest primary-ish data is Endor Labs.
- The phrase "the model will eat the harness" was not found verbatim in any source; the closest attributed statements are Noam Brown's "scaffolds will also just be replaced by the reasoning models" and the "crutch/拐杖" paraphrase.
